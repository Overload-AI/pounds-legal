Four things about the body-fat figure, stated plainly, because a number invites more confidence than this one deserves:

4. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE FEATURES AND THIRD-PARTY AI PROCESSORS

This Section is the most important part of this Policy. Please read it in full.

4.1 Which Features Are AI Features

The following features of the App are powered by artificial-intelligence models that we do not operate ourselves (each, an "AI Feature"):

  1. The coach — the conversational training assistant;
  2. Coach memory — the retention and retrieval of context across conversations;
  3. Progress-photo analysis — the body-composition assessment generated from a photograph you submit;
  4. AI-generated programs — training programs drafted or modified by the coach; and
  5. Coach-written notifications — the daily notification copy.

4.2 The Two Processors, and What Each Receives

We engage two independent providers. They receive different things, by different mechanisms, and the distinction matters.

(a) Anthropic PBC ("Anthropic") operates the models that generate the coach's replies, the progress-photo analysis, and the notification copy. Anthropic receives:

  • the text of the messages you send to the coach, and the coach's replies, including where they are re-submitted so that the coach can extract memories from a conversation or give a thread a title;
  • a contextual summary compiled from your account — your display name, your stated training goal, your unit preference, and the coach's stored memories about you, which may include goals, injuries, preferences, personal records, and observations about your habits — which accompanies each coach request;
  • the image data of a progress photograph, when and only when you request an analysis of that photograph. If you have previously had a photograph of the same pose analysed, the most recent such photograph is sent with it, so that the model can describe what has changed between the two. That earlier image is therefore transmitted again, on each later analysis of the same pose. A photograph you have never submitted for analysis is never sent;
  • your training data — recent workouts, personal records, your active program, and volume trends — when the coach retrieves it in order to answer a question you have asked; and
  • your display name, which appears in the instructions given to the model so that the coach can address you.

(b) OpenAI, L.P. ("OpenAI") operates the embedding model that powers coach memory retrieval. OpenAI receives:

  • the verbatim text of each message you send to the coach. This occurs on every coach message. The message is converted into a mathematical representation used to rank which of the coach's stored memories are most relevant to what you are discussing. The resulting representation is not used to compose the coach's reply;
  • the text of the coach's memories about you, including memories categorized as injuries, goals, and preferences; and
  • the narrative text describing your body composition that is derived from a progress photograph.

The image data of your progress photographs is not transmitted to OpenAI. A written description of your body composition, generated from that image, is.

(c) Notifications are a separate transmission, and it happens without you opening the App. If you have enabled notifications, the copy for them is written by Anthropic's model on a schedule. What is sent for that purpose is narrower than a coach turn, and we can state it exactly. Anthropic receives:

  • the hour at which you usually train, and whether you have trained today;
  • your current training streak, and how long it has been since your last weigh-in;
  • a summary of your recent workouts;
  • your protein target, if you have set one;
  • up to eight of the coach's stored memories about you, drawn from the body, goal, preference, and note categories — which can include the body-composition description derived from a progress photograph; and
  • which kinds of notification are to be written — for example, that a late-night-eating message or a missed-undertaking message is due. The underlying records are not sent. Your individual nightly-undertaking answers stay on our servers; only the resulting instruction to write that kind of message crosses.

Injury is deliberately treated differently on this path. Whether you have a live injury on record is used to decide which notification to send — an injured person resting on medical advice must not be told they are slacking — but the injury text itself is never given to the model that writes the notification, and cannot appear on your lock screen. Only the fact that an injury exists, as a yes-or-no, reaches that decision.

4.3 Health Information You Type Into the Coach

If you tell the coach about an injury, a symptom, a limitation, a medication, or any other health matter, that text is transmitted to Anthropic and to OpenAI along with the rest of your message.

We cannot determine in advance which of your messages contain health information, and we therefore cannot filter such information out. If there is something you would prefer not to disclose to those providers, do not enter it into the coach. Nothing in the manual tracker — your logged sets, your programs, your measurements, or your photographs — is transmitted to either provider unless you use an AI Feature.

4.4 Model Training

Under the commercial application-programming-interface terms on which we operate, neither Anthropic nor OpenAI is permitted to use content submitted through our account to train its models. We do not authorize such use, and we will not do so.

Model training and retention are separate questions, and the answers differ. Not training on your content does not mean not keeping it. Section 9.4 states how long each provider may keep what we send, including one exception that we cannot prevent and that bears directly on progress photographs. Read it before you use the photo-analysis feature.

Each provider's own privacy practices are described in its published privacy policy. We encourage you to read them.

Automated abuse detection at the providers. Both providers operate automated systems that inspect the content sent to them in order to detect misuse of their services. These systems are the providers' own. We do not operate them, cannot switch them off, cannot see their output, and are not told when they act.

We are telling you this here, rather than burying it, because of what the App sends. A progress photograph is frequently a photograph of a bare torso. Neither provider publishes the categories its automated systems detect, so we cannot tell you what those systems look for. Our own assessment is that such a photograph is more likely than ordinary text to attract an automated classifier's attention, and that a classifier can be wrong. The consequences of it being wrong are described in Section 9.4.

If you would rather not accept that risk, do not use progress-photo analysis. Photographs you store in the App but never submit for analysis are never sent to any AI provider. Every other feature of the App, including the coach, works without it.

4.5 Consent Is Required, and Is Enforced by Our Systems

No content is transmitted to Anthropic or to OpenAI unless you have affirmatively consented to this Policy. This is not a policy commitment alone; it is enforced by our backend. Every service function capable of transmitting your content to a third party checks your consent record before performing any work, and refuses the request outright if a current, unrevoked consent is not on file. A failure to verify your consent is treated as an absence of consent.

Your consent is recorded in an append-only ledger, together with the version of this Policy you agreed to and the point in the App at which you agreed. The record is written by our server, not by the App on your device, and it identifies you and the policy version from verified server-side values rather than from anything the App asserts.

4.6 Withdrawing Consent

You may withdraw your consent at any time in Settings. Withdrawal is effective prospectively. Upon withdrawal:

  • the coach, coach memory, progress-photo analysis, AI-generated programs, and coach-written notifications cease to function; and
  • every other feature of the App continues to work without change — logging workouts, running programs, viewing analytics and personal records, recording body measurements, and storing progress photos.

One narrow exception applies: if, before you withdrew consent, the coach proposed a change to your training data and you had not yet confirmed it, you may still confirm that pending change afterward. Confirming it transmits nothing to any third party; it applies a change already prepared and stored in your own account.

Withdrawal does not delete content already stored in your account, and it does not retrieve content previously transmitted to Anthropic or OpenAI — see Section 9.4 as to what those providers may retain. To obtain a copy of your content, see Section 10.1; to delete it, Section 10.2.

4.7 Re-Consent on Material Change

If we materially change this Policy, we will publish a new policy version, and the App will ask you to agree again before any AI Feature resumes operating. This is enforced by the same mechanism described in Section 4.5: the server compares the policy version you accepted against the current version and refuses transmission where they do not match.

9.4 Retention by the AI Providers — Including One Exception We Cannot Control

The periods in Section 9.3 govern our systems. Content transmitted to Anthropic or OpenAI under Section 4 is held on their systems, under their terms. This subsection states what we understand those terms to be. It is the part of this Policy we most want you to read, because one part of it is adverse to you and we do not control it.

(a) The default. Under the commercial application-programming-interface terms on which we operate, neither provider trains on the content we send (Section 4.4), and neither keeps that content as a durable record of you. Both, however, keep it briefly. Anthropic states that it automatically deletes application-programming-interface inputs and outputs within thirty days of receipt or generation. OpenAI states that it retains abuse-monitoring logs, which may contain prompts and responses, for up to thirty days. Both providers reserve the right to keep content longer where required by law or where necessary to enforce their usage policies. The thirty-day window is the ordinary case. The exception in paragraph (b) is not.

(b) The exception. Both providers run automated systems that classify the content sent to them in order to detect misuse of their services. Content that those automated systems flag may be retained by the provider — at Anthropic, for a period of up to two years — and may be reviewed by the provider's personnel during that period. Anthropic further states that where content is flagged it retains the trust-and-safety classification scores generated about that content for up to seven years — more than three times as long as the content itself. A score of that kind is a record about you, derived from what you sent.

We do not hold a zero-retention arrangement or a business-associate agreement with either provider, so the ordinary commercial terms described here are the terms that apply to your content. We note that Anthropic states that even under a zero-retention arrangement it continues to retain the results produced by its user-safety classifiers.

OpenAI receives text only, never a photograph (Section 4.2). OpenAI states that it logs application-programming-interface content for abuse monitoring for up to thirty days, and that it may retain content for longer where reasonably necessary to protect its services or any third party from harm. It publishes no outer limit on that longer period.

(c) Why we are telling you this in these terms. A progress photograph is often a photograph of a bare torso.

Neither provider publishes the categories its automated systems detect. Our own assessment — and we would rather state it than leave you to discover it — is that a photograph of a bare torso is more likely than ordinary text to attract the attention of an automated content classifier, and that such a classifier can be wrong. We cannot confirm that either provider operates a classifier of that kind, because neither says.

Anthropic's Usage Policy does not prohibit nudity; it prohibits the generation of sexually explicit material, which is not what the App does. The risk we are disclosing is therefore a risk of error, not a risk of enforcement: that an automated system flags an innocuous photograph anyway. If that happened, a photograph of your body could be retained for up to two years, and we would not be told, could not inspect the decision, and could not compel deletion.

(d) What we do control, and what we do not.
We control Whether anything is sent at all — nothing is, without your consent (Section 4.5). Which provider receives the image: the image data of your photographs goes to Anthropic only, never to OpenAI (Section 4.2). That the App's entire manual tracker works with the AI features switched off.
We do not control Whether a provider's automated systems flag your content. How long a provider retains flagged content. Whether a provider's personnel review it. Whether a provider deletes it when you delete your account with us.

(e) Deletion does not reach back. Deleting your account under Section 10.2 removes your data from our systems. It does not, and cannot, retrieve or delete content already transmitted to a provider, or content a provider has retained under paragraph (b). Once your account is deleted we transmit nothing further concerning you.

(f) If this is not acceptable to you, you have a real alternative. Decline or withdraw consent under Section 4.6, or simply never submit a photograph for analysis. Photographs you store in the App and do not submit for analysis are never transmitted to any AI provider, and the manual training tracker is unaffected.

10. GETTING A COPY OF YOUR DATA, AND DELETING YOUR ACCOUNT

Both of these are self-service. Neither requires an email to us, a support ticket, or a waiting period, and we do not charge for either.

10.1 Getting a Copy — Settings → Download my data

You may obtain a complete copy of everything we hold about you from within the App, at Settings → "Download my data."

The copy is assembled on our servers, from our database — not from whatever your phone happens to have synchronized. That distinction matters: your device holds only what it has pulled down, and a copy built from it would silently under-report. The file is machine-readable and includes, for each category, a row count, so that "we hold none of this" is distinguishable from "the export omitted it."

The copy contains your profile; your body measurements; your workout sessions and sets; your programs, program versions, program days, and program exercises; your personal records; the exercises you created; your progress-photograph records; your coach threads, messages, memories, and the log of actions the coach took on your data; your pending coach proposals; your nightly undertakings; your notification preferences; your crash and performance diagnostics; your subscription records; your consent history; and the per-message AI usage telemetry described in Section 3.3.

Progress photographs are files, not database rows. The copy therefore includes time-limited download links to your images rather than the images themselves. Those links expire seven days after the copy is generated, and the expiry timestamp is stated inside the file. Download the images within that period; you may generate a further copy at any time.

Three things are deliberately omitted, and the copy names each omission and why, so that you are not left unable to tell "not held" from "not included":

  • the numeric embeddings of your coach memories — roughly 1,500 numbers per memory, derived from the memory text, which is included in full, and meaningless on their own. We will include them on request;
  • the policy documents themselves, which are identical for every user and are not personal data; and
  • a legacy coach-persona record that is no longer read by any part of the App.

10.2 Deleting Your Account — Settings → Delete Account

You may delete your account and all of its contents from within the App, at Settings → Delete Account.

Deletion removes your account and every record attached to it — every workout session and set, every program and program version, every personal record, every body measurement, every progress photograph, every coach conversation, memory, and action log, every nightly undertaking, your consent history, your notification preferences, your subscription records, your crash diagnostics, and your profile — from our servers, and clears the corresponding data from your device. Your progress-photograph image files are purged from storage, and the purge is verified rather than assumed.

Deletion is permanent and cannot be undone. We cannot restore a deleted account. You may create a new account afterward, but nothing carries over. If you want a copy of your data, take it before you delete — use Section 10.1 first.

Content previously transmitted to Anthropic or OpenAI is subject to those providers' own retention practices, which we do not control and cannot reverse. See Section 9.4. We transmit nothing further concerning you once your account is deleted.

Deleting the App from your device does not delete your account. Cancelling your subscription does not delete your account, and deleting your account does not cancel your subscription — subscriptions are managed by Apple, and you must cancel yours in your Apple ID account settings.
